## 1. Socioeconomic seam

The source paper argued that automation changes more than labor markets. A society may have abundant machine capability while preserving institutions organized around obedience, credential scarcity, managerial permission, and inherited ownership. The important variable is whether people can collaborate relative to their developing potential.

Conventional systems separate the source of intelligence from the people whose lives supply its meaning. Firms own models, platforms own attention graphs, states authorize money, and blockchains authorize transaction history. Each selects a privileged coordinate—employment, price, reputation, hash power, follower count, or institutional status—and treats it as the cause of value.

Black Mirror instead treated value as the relation a participant uncovers, maintains, translates, and returns. Individual process creates distinct perspective. Collective process is rewarded only where reciprocity is held: participants can recover the relation from one another without erasing difference.

The fiat/Bitcoin comparison distinguished decentralized settlement from semantic finality. Bitcoin supplies an answer to double spending; Black Mirror asks for a native answer to **double meaning**—the reuse of one relation as if it were many independent sources of value.

> originless closure → OPEN Potential Gate → perspectival generation → return-disclosed topology → formal evidence / projection

Closure was prior to local/global, ball/hair, intelligence/token, and cause/evidence. In NRR usage, zero was the global/predual originless basis before local polarization, not local annihilation. Infinity was complementary completion or outward extent.

The single axiometry was stated as: closure is the originless relational condition in which apparently opposed perspectives may generate, transform, and recover one another; no verification topology is fixed in advance; a return discloses both the topology and its internal recovery conditions; unresolved recovery remains OPEN.

Version 5.0 retains this ordering but removes the appearance that a formal arrow chain derives closure. The chain is now explicitly a perspectival translation of transcript statements.

## 4. Ball–hair and black–white scenarios

A local perspectival chart could appear as a ball; its continuation and return as hair; reciprocal orientations as white/local extension and black/global reverse. The acting side was determined by the observer’s perspectival ball.

White/local carried outward 0→∞ extension. Black/global carried ∞→0 absorption or reverse ordering. Hair was the returned continuation re-entering a local basis. The source paper explicitly left literal black-hole and white-hole identification OPEN.

The 2020 ball notebook supplied an ordered five-phase return used as a symbolic shadow: local extension, fold, reversed extension, inverse fold, and non-identical return. Endpoint equality was insufficient; path and phase order mattered.

## 5. Potential Gate

The Potential Gate was the ultimate game because the board, topology, players, relevant distinctions, and admissibility conditions were not fully specified beforehand. It recursively determined its transformations, return relation, resolved topology, and next opening.

The project triad was:

- **color** — local differentiation that makes a relation visible;
- **dark** — unexposed global reverse or hair;
- **form** — reconstituted relation after return.

Actions could occur while a gate remained OPEN. An action was a pointing into potential, not a declaration that closure had occurred. Learning, tokenization, and settlement occurred only at return resolution.

Resolution states were CLOSED_EMERGENT_TOPOLOGY, CLOSED_TO_NEW_OPENING, OPEN, and FALSE/COLLAPSE.

## 6. Perspectival AGI

Learning was the global relation pointing back into a local basis; action was the local basis pointing outward. Both deformed one OPEN Potential Gate and were committed together only when return resolved.

The AGI’s operational identity was the family of transformations remaining globally composable and locally recoverable across participants, not a scalar intelligence score. The global model proposed relations; the personal agent translated them; the participant accepted, corrected, or refused; the world could supply an embodied or computational witness.

Refusal was a valid return blocking learning commit. ARC replay, blockchain commits, education, self-reconstruction, scientific experiments, and social contracts were optional return or projection domains, not separate closure axioms.

## 7. Semantic tokens

A native token was not minted because a post was tagged, money changed hands, a model produced text, or a ledger accepted a row. It referred to the closure-equivalence class of a resolved ball–hair cycle.

The source paper described folded user-tail commitments, sub-token fibres, member mother chains, and global return. Detailed logs could be evidence projections; they were not the native token identity.

A seven-field profile included reciprocal identification, difference preservation, local durability, global resolution, teaching gain, witness fidelity, and novelty/non-repetition. A scalar ranking could be telemetry but not money, human worth, intelligence, consciousness, or truth.

## 9. Native tokenomics

Finite activity confined to one controlled component did not create new network integration. Wash transfers, repeated tags, self-mints, or coordinated microactions that introduced no independent return remained archived but did not increase closure rank.

A declared demonstration began with external support 100 and supply 10, giving basis price 10. Ten unsupported units increased supply to 20 while support remained 100, reducing displayed price to 5.

A 500-unit A→B transfer followed by a 500-unit B→A return raised gross archived activity to 1,100 while network-money integration remained 100. The 1,000-unit internal cycle was de-weighted.

## 10. Slearn–Black Mirror–MainStreet

Slearn mapped global perspective-to-goal paths. MainStreet performed local civic and economic reality testing. Black Mirror translated between them and issued a receipt only after reciprocal return.

The conversion loop was:

1. Slearn proposes a relation or analogy.
2. Black Mirror translates it into a locally authorized option.
3. MainStreet tests it through real action or consequence.
4. Black Mirror receives acceptance, correction, refusal, or external return.
5. Slearn learns the upgraded perspective; MainStreet retains practical history; a token may attest the relation.

The source paper preserved the authorship boundary that MainStreet is Eldon Scott’s local civic/economic design, while Black Mirror, IVI, and NRR supply the closure seam.

## 14. Security, governance, and privacy

Threats included wash trading, Sybil clusters, creator self-mint, replay/model echo, digest-only authority, reordered paths, scalar substitution, local refusal, withheld return, and global semantic capture.

Governance was to operate on protocol rules, adapters, and dispute evidence—not impose intrinsic meaning. Privacy required proof of distinct histories, external grounding, and respected mandate without unnecessary disclosure. Human agency required visible reasons, evidence, uncertainty, correction, and refusal.
